# PART I — HAIR BIOLOGY FOUNDATIONS

## §1. Hair Follicle Anatomy

The hair follicle is a dynamic, regenerative mini-organ embedded in the dermis and, in its active state, the subcutis. Its canonical anatomical compartments are:

- **Infundibulum.** The uppermost segment from the skin surface to the sebaceous duct opening. Lined by epidermis-like epithelium; site of follicular ostium-level inflammation and follicular plugging.
- **Isthmus.** The segment between the sebaceous duct and the insertion of the arrector pili muscle. Contains the *bulge*, the principal reservoir of follicular epithelial stem cells.
- **Bulge.** Niche of follicular epithelial stem cells (keratin-15-expressing) and the seat of long-term follicular regenerative capacity. Damage to the bulge constitutes the principal anatomical determinant of irreversible alopecia.
- **Suprabulbar region.** The segment between the isthmus and the bulb; contains the outer and inner root sheaths.
- **Hair bulb.** The terminal expansion containing the dermal papilla and matrix keratinocytes. Locus of active proliferation during anagen.
- **Dermal papilla.** Mesenchymal signaling center that governs anagen entry, follicle size, hair shaft caliber, and anagen duration. Dermal papilla cell number is a principal determinant of follicle miniaturization status.
- **Matrix.** Highly proliferative epithelial compartment surrounding the dermal papilla; gives rise to the hair shaft and the inner root sheath.
- **Outer root sheath (ORS).** Continuous with the epidermis; contributes to the bulge stem cell pool and to perifollicular signaling.
- **Inner root sheath (IRS).** Molds the emerging hair shaft; disintegrates at the isthmus.
- **Hair shaft.** The keratinized, non-living product of the follicle, comprising medulla (variable), cortex, and cuticle.
- **Sebaceous gland.** Holocrine gland associated with each follicular unit; secretes sebum into the infundibulum.
- **Arrector pili muscle.** Smooth muscle inserting near the bulge; its degeneration is associated with permanent follicle loss in some scarring alopecias.
- **Perifollicular vasculature.** Capillary plexus supplying the dermal papilla; substrate of nutrient and oxygen delivery.
- **Perifollicular immune environment.** Includes resident immune cells and an immune-privileged zone around the anagen bulb. Collapse of immune privilege is central to alopecia areata.

## §2. Hair Cycle

The hair cycle is the recurrent biological program through which each follicle progresses across phases of growth, regression, rest, and shedding, followed by regeneration. Cycling is asynchronous across the scalp; at any moment, the scalp displays a mosaic of follicles in distinct phases.

### §2.1 Anagen
The active growth phase. Matrix proliferation, hair shaft elongation, and dermal papilla signaling are maximal. Scalp anagen duration is normally **2 to 7 years** and is the principal determinant of maximum achievable hair length and shaft caliber. **Anagen shortening** is a hallmark of androgenetic alopecia and of inflammatory follicular insults. Approximately **85–90%** of healthy scalp follicles are in anagen at any time.

### §2.2 Catagen
The brief regression phase. Matrix proliferation ceases, the lower follicle involutes, and the dermal papilla condenses and migrates upward toward the bulge. Duration is normally **2–3 weeks**. Catagen is largely a controlled apoptotic remodeling; pathological prolongation is uncommon.

### §2.3 Telogen
The resting phase. The follicle holds a club hair while the dermal papilla remains quiescent below the bulge. Duration is normally **2–4 months** on the scalp. Approximately **5–15%** of healthy scalp follicles are in telogen at any time. **Excess telogen entry** (telogen effluvium) shifts this ratio upward and increases shedding.

### §2.4 Exogen
The shedding phase. The club hair is released. Exogen is partially independent of telogen; a follicle may enter a new anagen with the club hair still in place, transiently producing two hairs from one follicle. Daily scalp exogen of **50–150 club hairs** is within normal variability.

### §2.5 Kenogen
A transient empty state — a follicle that has shed its club hair but has not yet re-entered anagen. Prolonged kenogen is a feature of advanced miniaturization and contributes to decreased hair density even when follicle count is preserved.

## §3. Biological Determinants of Healthy Hair Growth

- **Adequate dermal papilla signaling.** Sufficient dermal papilla cell number and intact paracrine signaling (Wnt/β-catenin, BMP modulation, IGF-1 axis).
- **Anagen duration.** Long anagen is the dominant driver of hair length and density.
- **Hair shaft caliber.** Function of dermal papilla volume, matrix activity, and keratinization quality.
- **Vascular supply.** Adequate perifollicular microcirculation, capillary patency, and oxygen delivery.
- **Nutrient sufficiency.** Adequate substrate for keratin synthesis (sulfur-containing amino acids, iron, zinc, biotin, B-complex, vitamin D, essential fatty acids, ferritin within physiologic range).
- **Endocrine balance.** Physiologic androgen, estrogen, thyroid, cortisol, and insulin signaling.
- **Immune homeostasis.** Intact follicular immune privilege; absence of chronic perifollicular inflammation.
- **Stem cell niche integrity.** Preserved bulge population and intact niche signaling.
- **Mechanical integrity.** Absence of chronic traction or repetitive trauma.
- **Cuticular and shaft integrity.** Adequate keratinization; minimal cosmetic injury.

## §4. Follicular Resilience

Follicular resilience is the capacity of the follicle to sustain or recover normal anagen, caliber, and density under physiologic stress. Resilience is a graded property, not binary. Principal determinants:

- Bulge stem cell reserve.
- Dermal papilla cellularity.
- Vascular and metabolic adequacy.
- Genetic susceptibility loci (especially androgen receptor sensitivity).
- Cumulative load of past insults (inflammatory, endocrine, nutritional, mechanical).
- Age (resilience declines progressively from the fourth decade onward).

## §5. Follicular Decline

Follicular decline is the progressive loss of resilience and function. Canonical decline categories:

- **Miniaturization.** Progressive reduction of dermal papilla volume, shaft caliber, and anagen duration. The hallmark of androgenetic alopecia; partially reversible if intervened early.
- **Cycling disruption.** Premature catagen entry, telogen excess, prolonged kenogen.
- **Inflammatory injury.** Perifollicular lymphocytic, neutrophilic, or granulomatous infiltrate, with or without fibrosis.
- **Fibrotic replacement.** Replacement of follicular and perifollicular tissue by scar; loss of bulge; irreversibility.
- **Niche exhaustion.** Cumulative attrition of bulge stem cells; functional irreversibility even without overt scarring.

# PART II — HAIR LOSS SIGNAL UNIVERSE

A signal is any clinically meaningful observable phenomenon that informs evaluation. This Part enumerates the canonical signal taxonomy. Severity, observability, and biological significance are declared per signal. Related and conflicting signals are declared to support downstream registry construction.

## §6. Patient-Reported Density and Volume Signals

### §6.1 Diffuse density reduction
- **Definition.** Patient-perceived overall thinning across the scalp.
- **Observability.** Patient-reported; visually corroborable.
- **Biological significance.** Suggestive of diffuse processes (telogen effluvium, diffuse AGA, endocrine, nutritional).
- **Severity dimensions.** Mild / moderate / severe; duration.
- **Related signals.** Excess shedding; widening part; reduced ponytail caliber.
- **Conflicting signals.** Strictly patterned recession with preserved diffuse density.

### §6.2 Widening of the central part
- **Definition.** Visible broadening of the midline parting.
- **Observability.** Visual.
- **Biological significance.** Hallmark of female-pattern hair loss and of diffuse miniaturization.
- **Severity dimensions.** Ludwig/Sinclair-class width categories.
- **Related signals.** Christmas-tree pattern at the frontal vertex; reduced midline density.
- **Conflicting signals.** Isolated bitemporal recession without midline change.

### §6.3 Reduced ponytail circumference
- **Definition.** Loss of bulk in the ponytail compared to historical baseline.
- **Observability.** Patient-reported; measurable.
- **Biological significance.** Integrates density and caliber loss.
- **Severity dimensions.** Proportional reduction.

## §7. Shedding Signals

### §7.1 Acute excess shedding
- **Definition.** Marked increase in daily club-hair loss persisting beyond ordinary variability, typically lasting weeks to months.
- **Observability.** Patient-reported.
- **Biological significance.** Characteristic of acute telogen effluvium following a triggering insult by ~2–4 months.
- **Severity dimensions.** Estimated daily count; duration; bilateral pull-test positivity.
- **Related signals.** Recent illness, parturition, surgery, severe stressor, medication change, weight loss.

### §7.2 Chronic excess shedding
- **Definition.** Persistent elevated shedding lasting ≥6 months.
- **Biological significance.** Chronic telogen effluvium; or compensated AGA with shedding as an ongoing feature; or sustained endocrine driver.
- **Severity dimensions.** Duration; symmetry.

### §7.3 Positive pull test
- **Definition.** Extraction of ≥10% of grasped hairs on gentle traction.
- **Observability.** Clinician-performed.
- **Biological significance.** Active telogen release or anagen anchoring failure.

### §7.4 Anagen-phase hair loss
- **Definition.** Shed hairs predominantly anagen (pigmented bulb, intact sheath) rather than telogen (club).
- **Biological significance.** Anagen effluvium; chemotherapy, severe inflammation, certain autoimmune processes.
- **Conflicting signals.** Pure club-hair shedding.

## §8. Pattern Signals

### §8.1 Bitemporal recession
- **Definition.** Recession of the frontal hairline at the temples.
- **Biological significance.** Hallmark of male-pattern AGA; less commonly female AGA with hyperandrogenism.
- **Severity dimensions.** Hamilton–Norwood categorical class.

### §8.2 Vertex thinning
- **Definition.** Thinning at the crown.
- **Biological significance.** Male-pattern AGA; advanced female AGA.
- **Severity dimensions.** Hamilton–Norwood categorical class.

### §8.3 Christmas-tree pattern
- **Definition.** Diffuse central thinning widening anteriorly with frontal hairline preservation.
- **Biological significance.** Female-pattern AGA.

### §8.4 Patchy alopecia
- **Definition.** Discrete circumscribed bald patches.
- **Biological significance.** Alopecia areata; tinea capitis; traction-related focal loss; trichotillomania.
- **Related signals.** Exclamation-mark hairs (areata); broken hairs of varying length (trichotillomania); scaling (tinea).

### §8.5 Ophiasis pattern
- **Definition.** Band-like loss along the occipital and temporal margins.
- **Biological significance.** Severe and treatment-resistant subtype of alopecia areata.

### §8.6 Diffuse alopecia totalis / universalis
- **Definition.** Loss of all scalp hair / all body hair.
- **Biological significance.** Most severe alopecia areata phenotypes.

### §8.7 Frontal fibrosing pattern
- **Definition.** Band-like frontotemporal recession with perifollicular erythema and loss of eyebrows.
- **Biological significance.** Frontal fibrosing alopecia (a primary lymphocytic scarring alopecia).
- **Severity dimensions.** Recession depth; eyebrow loss; lichenoid signs.

## §9. Scalp Signals

### §9.1 Perifollicular erythema
- **Definition.** Redness around follicular ostia.
- **Biological significance.** Active perifollicular inflammation; characteristic of lichen planopilaris, frontal fibrosing alopecia, and active AGA inflammatory subset.

### §9.2 Perifollicular scaling
- **Definition.** Keratotic scaling around follicular ostia.
- **Biological significance.** Lichen planopilaris; folliculitis decalvans; seborrheic dermatitis overlap.

### §9.3 Loss of follicular ostia
- **Definition.** Smooth, shiny scalp with absent follicular openings.
- **Biological significance.** **Cardinal sign of scarring alopecia.** Indicates irreversibility in affected zones.

### §9.4 Yellow dots
- **Definition.** Yellowish keratotic plugs in empty follicular openings (trichoscopy).
- **Biological significance.** Alopecia areata; advanced AGA.

### §9.5 Black dots
- **Definition.** Cadaverized hairs broken at the scalp surface.
- **Biological significance.** Active alopecia areata; trichotillomania; tinea capitis.

### §9.6 Exclamation-mark hairs
- **Definition.** Short, tapered hairs narrowed proximally.
- **Biological significance.** Pathognomonic for active alopecia areata.

### §9.7 Pustules
- **Definition.** Follicular pustules with or without crusting.
- **Biological significance.** Folliculitis decalvans; dissecting cellulitis; bacterial folliculitis.

### §9.8 Seborrheic scaling
- **Definition.** Greasy yellow scaling.
- **Biological significance.** Seborrheic dermatitis; may exacerbate shedding and amplify perifollicular inflammation.

### §9.9 Pruritus, burning, or trichodynia
- **Definition.** Subjective scalp symptoms.
- **Biological significance.** Active inflammation; seborrheic dermatitis; lichen planopilaris; FFA; some AGA cases.

### §9.10 Sebum dysregulation
- **Definition.** Marked seborrhea or marked xerosis.
- **Biological significance.** Endocrine influence; environmental contribution to scalp dysbiosis.

## §10. Caliber and Shaft Signals

### §10.1 Hair shaft caliber reduction
- **Definition.** Reduction in average shaft diameter relative to baseline.
- **Biological significance.** Miniaturization; hallmark of AGA.

### §10.2 Caliber heterogeneity (anisotrichosis)
- **Definition.** Coexistence of vellus, intermediate, and terminal hairs within a small scalp field.
- **Biological significance.** Trichoscopic hallmark of AGA. **>20% diameter variability** is a canonical AGA criterion.

### §10.3 Increased vellus proportion
- **Definition.** Elevated proportion of fine, short, lightly pigmented hairs.
- **Biological significance.** Advanced miniaturization.

### §10.4 Brittle or fragile shafts
- **Definition.** Easy breakage, split ends, reduced tensile resilience.
- **Biological significance.** Nutritional deficiency; thyroid dysfunction; cosmetic injury; rare shaft disorders.

### §10.5 Structural shaft anomalies
- **Definition.** Trichorrhexis nodosa, pili torti, monilethrix, trichoptilosis.
- **Biological significance.** Inherited shaft disorders; severe nutritional, endocrine, or cosmetic insults.

## §11. Hairline and Density Mapping Signals

### §11.1 Frontal hairline preservation
- **Definition.** Maintenance of the anterior hairline despite midline thinning.
- **Biological significance.** Female-pattern AGA; distinguishing feature from FFA.

### §11.2 Frontal hairline recession with lonely-hair sign
- **Definition.** Isolated terminal hairs in front of the receded line.
- **Biological significance.** Frontal fibrosing alopecia.

### §11.3 Occipital sparing
- **Definition.** Preserved density in the occipital donor zone.
- **Biological significance.** AGA; defines surgical donor adequacy.

### §11.4 Diffuse loss with occipital involvement
- **Definition.** Thinning extending into the occipital region.
- **Biological significance.** Diffuse processes (TE, endocrine, nutritional, immune-mediated); reduces surgical donor adequacy.

## §12. Systemic and Contextual Signals

### §12.1 Menstrual irregularity
- **Biological significance.** Suggestive of PCOS, hyperandrogenism, or hypothalamic dysfunction.

### §12.2 Hirsutism or virilization
- **Biological significance.** Hyperandrogenism; PCOS; rarely adrenal pathology.

### §12.3 Galactorrhea
- **Biological significance.** Hyperprolactinemia; secondary endocrine effect on cycling.

### §12.4 Cold intolerance, weight change, fatigue
- **Biological significance.** Thyroid dysfunction (hypo- or hyper-).

### §12.5 Recent acute illness, surgery, parturition, severe weight loss, severe psychological stressor
- **Biological significance.** Trigger window for acute telogen effluvium at ~8–16 weeks.

### §12.6 Medication initiation or cessation
- **Biological significance.** Wide range of medications can induce telogen or anagen effluvium; hormonal contraceptive changes; isotretinoin; certain antiepileptics, anticoagulants, antithyroid agents, lithium, oncologic therapies.

### §12.7 Nutritional inadequacy
- **Biological significance.** Iron deficiency (with or without anemia), zinc deficiency, vitamin D inadequacy, protein-calorie inadequacy, severe restrictive dieting.

### §12.8 Mechanical history
- **Biological significance.** Tight hairstyles, repetitive tension, helmet/hat compression, chemical relaxers, cosmetic over-processing.

## §13. Signal Conflict Rules

When two signals are biologically incompatible, the system must record the conflict explicitly and defer resolution to the cause-evaluation layer. Canonical conflict examples:

- Loss of follicular ostia ⟂ Preserved miniaturization-pattern density.
- Pure ophiasis pattern ⟂ Bitemporal–vertex Hamilton–Norwood pattern.
- Frontal fibrosing band ⟂ Frontal hairline preservation.
- Pure anagen effluvium ⟂ Pure telogen shedding profile.

# PART III — BIOLOGICAL PATHWAY UNIVERSE

A pathway is a coherent biological mechanism that integrates signals and converts them into mechanistic load relevant to one or more causes. Each pathway is enumerated with role, activation triggers, supporting signals, suppressing signals, expected consequences, and reversibility.

## §14. Androgen Signaling Pathway

- **Biological role.** DHT-mediated miniaturization of genetically susceptible follicles via androgen receptor activation in dermal papilla.
- **Activation triggers.** Genetic AR sensitivity; elevated 5α-reductase activity; elevated free androgen; postmenopausal estrogen decline unmasking baseline androgen exposure.
- **Supporting signals.** Bitemporal recession; vertex thinning; Christmas-tree pattern; caliber heterogeneity; hirsutism; menstrual irregularity.
- **Suppressing signals.** Loss of follicular ostia (suggests scarring rather than AGA); strict ophiasis; exclamation-mark hairs.
- **Expected consequences.** Progressive miniaturization, anagen shortening, caliber reduction, density loss in pattern distribution.
- **Reversibility.** Partial; reversibility decreases with degree of miniaturization and duration; once vellus and kenogen dominate, recovery ceiling is reduced.

## §15. Inflammatory Perifollicular Pathway (Non-Scarring Subset)

- **Biological role.** Low-grade lymphohistiocytic perifollicular inflammation amplifying miniaturization without overt fibrosis.
- **Activation triggers.** Sebaceous overactivity; *Malassezia* overgrowth; microbiome disturbance; chronic mechanical irritation.
- **Supporting signals.** Pruritus, burning, trichodynia; perifollicular erythema without ostia loss; seborrheic scaling.
- **Suppressing signals.** Loss of follicular ostia (scarring); pustular crusting (suggests bacterial pathway).
- **Expected consequences.** Amplification of miniaturization; increased shedding.
- **Reversibility.** Largely reversible if controlled before structural injury.

## §16. Cicatricial (Scarring) Inflammatory Pathway

- **Biological role.** Sustained lymphocytic, neutrophilic, or mixed inflammation directed at the bulge, resulting in stem cell loss and fibrosis.
- **Subtypes.**
  - **Lymphocytic primary scarring:** lichen planopilaris, frontal fibrosing alopecia, central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia, pseudopelade of Brocq.
  - **Neutrophilic primary scarring:** folliculitis decalvans, dissecting cellulitis.
  - **Mixed:** acne keloidalis nuchae, folliculitis (chronic).
- **Activation triggers.** Autoimmune or autoinflammatory targeting of bulge; chronic bacterial colonization; mechanical/chemical injury in CCCA.
- **Supporting signals.** Perifollicular erythema, perifollicular scaling, loss of follicular ostia, pustules, lonely-hair sign (FFA), symptoms (pruritus, pain, burning).
- **Suppressing signals.** None — these signals dominate when present.
- **Expected consequences.** Irreversible focal loss in affected zones; ongoing extension if uncontrolled.
- **Reversibility.** **Irreversible in already-scarred zones.** Disease activity may be stabilized; lost density is not restored.

## §17. Telogen-Shift Pathway

- **Biological role.** Premature anagen exit driven by systemic stressor producing synchronized telogen entry of a large follicle cohort, manifesting clinically ~8–16 weeks later.
- **Activation triggers.** Acute illness, fever, surgery, parturition, severe psychological stress, severe weight loss, medication change, anesthesia, abrupt hormonal change, severe nutritional deficit.
- **Supporting signals.** Acute excess shedding; positive bilateral pull test of telogen clubs; recent stressor in the preceding 2–4 months; diffuse density reduction without pattern.
- **Suppressing signals.** Loss of follicular ostia; strict pattern distribution without diffuse component; anagen-bulb shedding.
- **Expected consequences.** Discrete shedding episode of 3–6 months; density returns toward baseline once trigger resolves.
- **Reversibility.** Fully reversible in acute self-limited form; chronic telogen effluvium has slower and more variable resolution.

## §18. Anagen-Arrest Pathway

- **Biological role.** Acute interruption of anagen matrix proliferation producing immediate fracture or release of anagen-phase hairs.
- **Activation triggers.** Cytotoxic chemotherapy; severe acute inflammation; severe protein-energy malnutrition; some autoimmune insults.
- **Supporting signals.** Anagen-phase hair loss; rapid, severe, often near-total shedding within days to weeks; pigmented bulbs on shed hairs.
- **Suppressing signals.** Pure telogen clubs; chronic timeline.
- **Expected consequences.** Often dramatic but typically reversible once insult resolves, given intact follicular niche.
- **Reversibility.** Largely reversible if the bulge remains intact.

## §19. Autoimmune Follicular Pathway (Non-Scarring)

- **Biological role.** Loss of follicular immune privilege producing peribulbar lymphocytic attack on anagen follicles, with preserved follicular ostia and preserved stem cell niche.
- **Activation triggers.** Genetic predisposition; environmental triggers; stress; comorbid autoimmunity (thyroid, vitiligo, atopy).
- **Supporting signals.** Patchy alopecia; exclamation-mark hairs; ophiasis; tapered hairs; yellow and black dots; rapid progression; nail pitting; eyebrow/lash involvement; alopecia totalis/universalis.
- **Suppressing signals.** Loss of follicular ostia; pattern distribution; nutrient deficiency–dominant profile.
- **Expected consequences.** Reversible follicular shutdown; relapse-remitting course; spontaneous remission possible.
- **Reversibility.** Largely reversible at the follicular level; however, extensive and chronic disease has lower regrowth probability.

## §20. Endocrine Modulation Pathways

### §20.1 Thyroid axis
- **Biological role.** Thyroid hormone modulates anagen entry, cycling speed, and shaft caliber.
- **Activation triggers.** Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism.
- **Supporting signals.** Diffuse thinning; brittle shafts; lateral eyebrow thinning; systemic thyroid signs.
- **Reversibility.** Largely reversible with euthyroid restoration.

### §20.2 Hyperprolactinemia
- **Biological role.** Prolactin elevation suppresses follicular cycling and amplifies androgenic effects.
- **Supporting signals.** Galactorrhea; menstrual irregularity; diffuse thinning.
- **Reversibility.** Reversible with cause control.

### §20.3 PCOS / hyperandrogenism
- **Biological role.** Sustained androgen exposure amplifies the androgen-signaling pathway and provides a continuous endocrine driver.
- **Supporting signals.** Hirsutism; acne; menstrual irregularity; central adiposity; insulin resistance markers.
- **Reversibility.** Partially reversible at the endocrine level; AGA component reversibility depends on miniaturization stage.

### §20.4 Postpartum and perimenopausal hormonal transitions
- **Biological role.** Abrupt estrogen change synchronizes telogen entry and unmasks underlying androgen susceptibility.
- **Supporting signals.** Diffuse postpartum shedding at ~3–4 months; midlife diffuse thinning with pattern features.
- **Reversibility.** Postpartum: largely reversible. Perimenopausal: dependent on underlying AGA biology.

### §20.5 Insulin resistance / metabolic syndrome
- **Biological role.** Hyperinsulinemia raises ovarian androgen output and reduces SHBG; metabolic milieu amplifies miniaturization.
- **Reversibility.** Reversibility of endocrine driver; AGA reversibility partial.

## §21. Nutritional and Metabolic Pathway

- **Biological role.** Substrate insufficiency for keratinization, follicular cycling, and matrix proliferation.
- **Key deficiencies.**
  - **Iron / ferritin.** Iron deficiency impairs cycling; ferritin below physiologic mid-range associates with telogen effluvium and impaired regrowth.
  - **Zinc.** Required for keratin synthesis; deficiency causes shedding and brittle hair.
  - **Vitamin D.** Modulates hair cycling via the vitamin D receptor in keratinocytes; insufficiency associates with alopecia areata and telogen effluvium.
  - **Biotin.** True deficiency is rare and causes overt hair and skin findings; supraphysiologic supplementation lacks evidence in non-deficient individuals.
  - **Protein-energy adequacy.** Severe restriction induces telogen and anagen disruption.
  - **Essential fatty acids.** Deficiency impairs scalp and shaft integrity.
  - **Vitamin B12, folate, vitamin A excess or deficiency, selenium toxicity or deficiency.** Each can disrupt cycling.
- **Reversibility.** Highly reversible with adequacy restoration, given an intact follicular niche.

## §22. Vascular and Microcirculatory Pathway

- **Biological role.** Adequacy of perifollicular blood flow and oxygenation.
- **Activation triggers.** Smoking; chronic microvascular disease; severe systemic illness.
- **Supporting signals.** Diffuse caliber reduction; slowed regrowth; coexistence with other risk factors.
- **Reversibility.** Partial; cumulative microvascular injury may be only partially reversible.

## §23. Microbiome and Scalp-Surface Pathway

- **Biological role.** Imbalance of scalp commensal flora (notably *Malassezia*, *Cutibacterium*, *Staphylococcus*) amplifies perifollicular inflammation and shedding.
- **Activation triggers.** Seborrheic dermatitis; chronic occlusion; harsh surfactant exposure; cosmetic product overuse.
- **Supporting signals.** Seborrheic scaling; pruritus; perifollicular erythema; flaring with stress.
- **Reversibility.** Largely reversible with control of dysbiosis and barrier restoration.

## §24. Mechanical and Cosmetic Injury Pathway

- **Biological role.** Repetitive traction or chemical/thermal injury producing follicular and shaft damage.
- **Activation triggers.** Tight braids, extensions, weaves; high ponytails; chemical relaxers; bleach; high-heat styling; trichotillomania.
- **Supporting signals.** Marginal recession; broken hairs of varying length; shaft fragility; perifollicular pustules in advanced traction.
- **Reversibility.** Reversible early; irreversible once traction alopecia transitions to scarred zones.

## §25. Psychogenic and Behavioral Pathway

- **Biological role.** Chronic stress activates HPA axis, modulates immune function, amplifies telogen-shift and autoimmune pathways; behavioral pulling (trichotillomania) produces direct follicular injury.
- **Reversibility.** Pathway driver reversible; downstream injuries variably reversible.

# PART IV — ROOT CAUSE UNIVERSE

A root cause is a biologically distinct etiology of hair loss recognized by HairOS. Causes are enumerated with biological basis, signal profile, pathway profile, competing causes, exclusion criteria, and progression characteristics. Multifactorial causes are explicit.

## §26. Androgenetic Alopecia (Male Pattern)
- **Biological basis.** Genetically determined AR-mediated miniaturization in pattern distribution.
- **Signal profile.** Bitemporal recession; vertex thinning; caliber heterogeneity; occipital sparing.
- **Pathway profile.** Androgen signaling dominant; inflammatory perifollicular subset variably present.
- **Competing causes.** Diffuse alopecias mimicking pattern AGA; early scarring alopecias.
- **Exclusion criteria.** Loss of follicular ostia; ophiasis; lonely-hair sign.
- **Progression.** Slow, decadal; staged by Hamilton–Norwood.

## §27. Androgenetic Alopecia (Female Pattern)
- **Biological basis.** Genetic AR sensitivity; modulated by endocrine context (PCOS, menopause).
- **Signal profile.** Central part widening; Christmas-tree pattern; frontal hairline preservation; caliber heterogeneity.
- **Pathway profile.** Androgen signaling; endocrine modulation; perifollicular inflammatory subset variable.
- **Competing causes.** Chronic telogen effluvium; thyroid; FFA (distinguished by hairline behavior).
- **Exclusion criteria.** Frontal recession with lonely hairs; loss of ostia.
- **Progression.** Slow; staged by Ludwig/Sinclair.

## §28. Acute Telogen Effluvium
- **Biological basis.** Synchronized telogen entry triggered by acute systemic insult.
- **Signal profile.** Acute excess shedding; positive pull test; antecedent trigger 2–4 months prior; diffuse density loss without pattern.
- **Pathway profile.** Telogen-shift dominant.
- **Competing causes.** Acute AGA flare; chronic TE; nutritional cause; thyroid; medication.
- **Exclusion criteria.** Pattern distribution; ostia loss; anagen-bulb shedding.
- **Progression.** Self-limited; recovery in 6–12 months in the absence of ongoing trigger.

## §29. Chronic Telogen Effluvium
- **Biological basis.** Persistent or recurrent telogen-shift without strict pattern; often female, midlife.
- **Signal profile.** Persistent diffuse shedding ≥6 months; mild bitemporal recession may coexist; normal scalp.
- **Pathway profile.** Telogen-shift; often endocrine or nutritional modulators.
- **Competing causes.** Diffuse AGA; thyroid; nutritional; autoimmune.
- **Exclusion criteria.** Ostia loss; strict pattern; anagen-bulb predominance.
- **Progression.** Fluctuating, often years.

## §30. Anagen Effluvium
- **Biological basis.** Acute interruption of anagen matrix proliferation.
- **Signal profile.** Rapid severe shedding; anagen-bulb hairs.
- **Pathway profile.** Anagen-arrest dominant.
- **Competing causes.** Severe TE; severe autoimmune onset.
- **Progression.** Resolves with cessation of insult; near-complete regrowth typical.

## §31. Alopecia Areata
- **Biological basis.** Loss of follicular immune privilege producing peribulbar lymphocytic attack.
- **Signal profile.** Patchy alopecia; exclamation-mark hairs; nail pitting; ophiasis or universalis in severe forms.
- **Pathway profile.** Autoimmune follicular (non-scarring) dominant.
- **Competing causes.** Tinea capitis; trichotillomania; congenital triangular alopecia.
- **Exclusion criteria.** Loss of follicular ostia.
- **Progression.** Relapse-remitting; unpredictable; spontaneous remission possible.

## §32. Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia
- **Biological basis.** Lymphocytic primary cicatricial alopecia targeting the bulge, predilection for frontal hairline and eyebrows.
- **Signal profile.** Band-like frontotemporal recession; lonely-hair sign; perifollicular erythema and scaling; eyebrow loss; loss of follicular ostia in the band.
- **Pathway profile.** Cicatricial inflammatory dominant.
- **Competing causes.** Female AGA (preserved hairline); traction alopecia (history).
- **Exclusion criteria.** Preserved ostia; diffuse non-pattern thinning.
- **Progression.** Slow but progressive; irreversible in scarred zones; stabilization is the realistic objective.

## §33. Lichen Planopilaris
- **Biological basis.** Lymphocytic primary cicatricial alopecia, often patchy multifocal involvement.
- **Signal profile.** Perifollicular erythema and scaling; pruritus, burning; loss of ostia in affected patches.
- **Pathway profile.** Cicatricial inflammatory.
- **Progression.** Stabilizable; not reversible in scarred zones.

## §34. Central Centrifugal Cicatricial Alopecia (CCCA)
- **Biological basis.** Lymphocytic scarring alopecia centered at the vertex, with strong demographic predilection and mechanical/chemical contributors.
- **Signal profile.** Central vertex smoothness with loss of ostia; mild symptoms; surrounding intact hair.
- **Pathway profile.** Cicatricial inflammatory; mechanical/cosmetic contribution.
- **Progression.** Centrifugal extension; stabilizable.

## §35. Folliculitis Decalvans
- **Biological basis.** Neutrophilic primary cicatricial alopecia with chronic staphylococcal colonization.
- **Signal profile.** Pustules; crusting; tufted hairs (multiple emerging from a single ostium); loss of ostia in advanced zones.
- **Pathway profile.** Cicatricial inflammatory (neutrophilic); microbiome contribution.
- **Progression.** Chronic; stabilizable; not reversible in scarred zones.

## §36. Dissecting Cellulitis of the Scalp
- **Biological basis.** Chronic neutrophilic perifollicular inflammation with sinus tracts and abscesses.
- **Signal profile.** Boggy nodules, fluctuant areas, sinuses, scarring.
- **Progression.** Chronic; stabilizable; high scarring burden.

## §37. Traction Alopecia
- **Biological basis.** Sustained mechanical tension on follicles producing perifollicular inflammation and eventual scarring.
- **Signal profile.** Marginal recession; broken hairs; pustular phase possible; late ostia loss.
- **Pathway profile.** Mechanical injury; secondary cicatricial in late stage.
- **Progression.** Reversible early; irreversible once scarred.

## §38. Trichotillomania
- **Biological basis.** Behavioral pulling producing irregular patches with broken hairs of varying length and intact ostia.
- **Signal profile.** Bizarre or geometric patches; broken hairs at varying lengths; absent exclamation-mark hairs; no scaling.
- **Progression.** Reversible at the follicular level if behavior resolves; chronic cases may produce scarring.

## §39. Tinea Capitis
- **Biological basis.** Dermatophyte infection of follicles.
- **Signal profile.** Patchy alopecia with scaling; broken hairs; black dots; possible kerion (boggy inflammatory mass).
- **Progression.** Reversible with antifungal therapy; severe inflammatory forms may scar.

## §40. Thyroid Dysfunction-Related Hair Loss
- **Biological basis.** Hyper- or hypothyroid disruption of cycling.
- **Signal profile.** Diffuse thinning; lateral eyebrow thinning; brittle shafts; systemic signs.
- **Pathway profile.** Endocrine modulation (thyroid).
- **Progression.** Reversible with euthyroid restoration; lags by 3–6 months.

## §41. Hyperprolactinemia-Related Hair Loss
- **Biological basis.** Prolactin elevation suppresses cycling.
- **Signal profile.** Diffuse thinning; galactorrhea; menstrual irregularity.
- **Progression.** Reversible with cause control.

## §42. PCOS-Associated Hair Loss
- **Biological basis.** Multifactorial: hyperandrogenism, insulin resistance, inflammation amplifying AGA susceptibility.
- **Signal profile.** Female-pattern thinning; hirsutism; acne; menstrual irregularity; metabolic features.
- **Pathway profile.** Androgen signaling + endocrine modulation + AGA biology.
- **Progression.** Partial reversibility of endocrine driver; AGA partial.

## §43. Postpartum Effluvium
- **Biological basis.** Synchronized telogen entry following postpartum estrogen withdrawal.
- **Signal profile.** Diffuse shedding at ~3–4 months postpartum.
- **Progression.** Reversible by 6–12 months in most cases.

## §44. Drug-Induced Hair Loss
- **Biological basis.** Specific drug class effect on cycling (telogen shift, anagen arrest, sebaceous suppression, hormonal modulation).
- **Signal profile.** Onset temporally linked to drug initiation or change; diffuse pattern; relevant medication review.
- **Progression.** Reversible with drug cessation in most classes; some persistent effects possible.

## §45. Nutritional Deficiency-Related Hair Loss
- **Biological basis.** Substrate insufficiency.
- **Signal profile.** Diffuse thinning; brittle shafts; pallor; relevant dietary history; laboratory confirmation.
- **Progression.** Reversible with restoration.

## §46. Seborrheic Dermatitis–Amplified Loss
- **Biological basis.** Microbiome dysbiosis amplifying perifollicular inflammation.
- **Signal profile.** Seborrheic scaling; pruritus; mild shedding; underlying AGA often present.
- **Progression.** Reversible component; underlying AGA biology unchanged.

## §47. Aging-Related Senescent Alopecia
- **Biological basis.** Niche exhaustion and dermal papilla attrition with age, independent of pattern AGA.
- **Signal profile.** Diffuse caliber reduction in late decades; no strict pattern; no inflammation.
- **Progression.** Slow; partially modifiable.

## §48. Multifactorial Causes (Recognized Composites)

HairOS explicitly recognizes multifactorial composites where two or more causes act concurrently. Composite logic must declare a compositeRule and reciprocal eligibility, per Phase 5B §28 and §29. Canonical composites:

- **AGA + Chronic Telogen Effluvium.**
- **AGA + Female Endocrine Driver (PCOS, perimenopausal, postpartum).**
- **AGA + Seborrheic Dermatitis Amplification.**
- **AGA + Nutritional Insufficiency.**
- **Traction + Cicatricial Conversion.**
- **Alopecia Areata + Thyroid Autoimmunity.**
- **FFA + AGA Background.**
- **Drug-Induced + Underlying AGA.**

For every composite, the contribution of each constituent must be evaluable independently before composite assertion.

# PART V — MECHANISM KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

The mechanism graph encodes canonical chains:

> **Cause → Mechanism → Follicular Impact → Clinical Consequence**

This Part declares canonical chains for every major cause category and notes shared, convergent, and irreversible mechanisms.

## §49. Androgenetic Alopecia Chain
- **Cause:** AGA (susceptible genotype).
- **Mechanism:** DHT-AR binding in dermal papilla → suppression of pro-anagen signaling (Wnt/β-catenin downregulation, IGF-1 modulation, TGF-β upregulation).
- **Follicular Impact:** Anagen shortening; dermal papilla cell loss; terminal-to-vellus transition.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Pattern miniaturization; reduced density; reduced caliber; pattern distribution.

## §50. Acute Telogen Effluvium Chain
- **Cause:** Systemic insult.
- **Mechanism:** Premature catagen entry across a cohort of follicles within days of insult; synchronized telogen pool.
- **Follicular Impact:** Cohort shed at ~2–4 months as exogen releases club hairs.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Acute diffuse shedding; transient density loss; recovery toward baseline.

## §51. Chronic Telogen Effluvium Chain
- **Cause:** Persistent or recurrent driver.
- **Mechanism:** Repeated or sustained telogen shifts; possible shortening of anagen.
- **Follicular Impact:** Persistent elevation of telogen fraction; subtle density loss.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Chronic diffuse shedding; modest density reduction; fluctuating course.

## §52. Anagen Effluvium Chain
- **Cause:** Cytotoxic or severe inflammatory insult.
- **Mechanism:** Acute arrest of matrix proliferation → shaft fracture or anagen release.
- **Follicular Impact:** Bulk anagen loss; preserved bulge if insult is non-scarring.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Rapid extensive shedding; rapid regrowth after recovery.

## §53. Alopecia Areata Chain
- **Cause:** AA.
- **Mechanism:** Collapse of follicular immune privilege → peribulbar CD8+ T-cell attack on anagen follicles → IFN-γ–driven follicular shutdown.
- **Follicular Impact:** Forced catagen; preserved stem cells; possible re-entry.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Patchy non-scarring loss; reversible at follicle level; relapse-remitting.

## §54. Lymphocytic Cicatricial Chain (LPP / FFA / CCCA)
- **Cause:** Primary lymphocytic scarring alopecia.
- **Mechanism:** Sustained lymphocytic attack centered on the bulge; sebaceous gland loss; fibrotic replacement.
- **Follicular Impact:** Bulge depletion; permanent loss of regenerative capacity in affected ostia.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Irreversible focal loss; centrifugal or band-like extension if uncontrolled.

## §55. Neutrophilic Cicatricial Chain (Folliculitis Decalvans / Dissecting Cellulitis)
- **Cause:** Neutrophilic primary cicatricial alopecia.
- **Mechanism:** Chronic neutrophilic perifollicular inflammation; bacterial colonization; abscess and sinus formation in dissecting cellulitis.
- **Follicular Impact:** Bulge destruction; tufting; scarring.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Irreversible patchy loss; chronic active disease.

## §56. Traction → Cicatricial Conversion Chain
- **Cause:** Sustained mechanical tension.
- **Mechanism:** Perifollicular inflammation → repeated injury → eventual fibrosis.
- **Follicular Impact:** Early: reversible follicular stress. Late: bulge loss.
- **Clinical Consequence:** Reversible marginal thinning in early phase; irreversible scarred margin in late phase.

## §57. Endocrine Modulation Chains

### §57.1 Thyroid chain
Thyroid dysfunction → altered cycling kinetics → diffuse thinning and brittle shafts → reversible with euthyroid restoration.

### §57.2 Hyperprolactinemia chain
Elevated prolactin → cycling suppression → diffuse thinning → reversible with cause control.

### §57.3 PCOS chain
Hyperandrogenism + insulin resistance → amplified androgen signaling and inflammatory milieu → pattern miniaturization with diffuse features → partial reversibility.

### §57.4 Postpartum chain
Estrogen withdrawal → synchronized catagen entry → 3–4 month diffuse shedding → typically reversible.

### §57.5 Perimenopausal chain
Estrogen decline → unmasking of androgen susceptibility → female-pattern progression → partial reversibility depending on stage.

## §58. Nutritional Chain
Substrate insufficiency → impaired keratinization and cycling → telogen excess, brittle shafts → reversible with restoration if niche intact.

## §59. Microbiome / Seborrheic Chain
*Malassezia* and lipid dysbiosis → perifollicular inflammation → amplified shedding and miniaturization in susceptible follicles → reversible component superimposed on underlying biology.

## §60. Drug-Induced Chains
Drug class–specific cycling disruption → telogen excess or anagen arrest → diffuse loss → reversible in most classes with cessation.

## §61. Shared Mechanisms
- **Perifollicular lymphocytic inflammation** is shared across AGA inflammatory subset, AA, LPP, FFA, CCCA, but with distinct targets and outcomes (non-scarring vs. scarring).
- **Cycling synchronization** is shared across acute TE, postpartum, drug-induced, and severe systemic insult.
- **Androgen sensitivity** is shared across male AGA, female AGA, and PCOS-amplified AGA.

## §62. Convergent Mechanisms
- **Miniaturization** is the convergent end-state of AGA biology, PCOS-amplified AGA, perimenopausal unmasking, and aging-related senescence — each via different upstream drivers.
- **Diffuse shedding** is the convergent presentation of acute TE, chronic TE, thyroid, postpartum, drug-induced, nutritional, and chronic stress causes.

## §63. Irreversible Mechanisms
The following mechanisms are biologically irreversible at the follicular level once realized:

- **Bulge depletion** in scarred zones (LPP, FFA, CCCA, folliculitis decalvans, dissecting cellulitis, advanced traction).
- **Fibrotic replacement** of follicular and perifollicular tissue.
- **Niche exhaustion** in advanced senescent alopecia.
- **Permanent dermal papilla cell loss** below a threshold incompatible with terminal-hair production.

For any zone exhibiting these mechanisms, recovery of density is not a defensible objective; stabilization is.

# PART VI — CLINICAL STATE MODEL

## §64. Severity Concepts
Severity is graded along multiple dimensions, not a single scalar:

- **Extent.** Proportion of scalp affected; pattern stage (Hamilton–Norwood, Ludwig/Sinclair, FFA band depth, SALT score for AA).
- **Activity.** Presence of active inflammation, ongoing shedding, expanding patches, or recent progression.
- **Structural injury.** Presence and extent of loss of ostia, scarring, or fibrotic replacement.
- **Functional impact.** Patient-reported impact on quality of life and self-perception (recorded but not used to modify biological assertions).

## §65. Progression Concepts
Progression is a temporal property:

- **Stable.** No meaningful change across a defined reassessment interval.
- **Slowly progressive.** Documented worsening over months to years.
- **Rapidly progressive.** Documented worsening over weeks.
- **Relapsing.** Episodes of activity interspersed with stability (AA, LPP).
- **Self-limited.** Discrete episode expected to resolve (acute TE, anagen effluvium with insult resolution).

## §66. Stability Concepts
Stability is determined by:

- Absence of active inflammation signs.
- Absence of expanding scarred zones.
- Absence of accelerating shedding.
- Maintained density and caliber across two reassessments separated by a defined interval per the Monitoring Registry.

Stability is not equivalent to absence of disease; it is absence of active progression.

## §67. Recovery Concepts
Recovery refers to measurable improvement toward a biologically attainable ceiling:

- **Density recovery.** Increase in terminal hair count per unit area.
- **Caliber recovery.** Increase in average shaft diameter.
- **Cycling recovery.** Normalization of telogen fraction and shedding rate.
- **Symptomatic recovery.** Resolution of pruritus, burning, trichodynia, scaling, erythema.

Recovery is bounded by the reversibility ceiling of the underlying cause (Part VIII).

# PART VII — TREATMENT OBJECTIVE FRAMEWORK

A treatment objective is a biologically valid goal whose realization is the target of intervention. Objectives are declared with purpose, biological target, and expected effect. Objectives are biologically grounded; they are not marketing positions.

## §68. Reduce Androgen Impact on Susceptible Follicles
- **Purpose.** Slow or reverse AGA miniaturization.
- **Biological target.** DHT availability or AR signaling at the dermal papilla.
- **Expected effect.** Stabilization or partial reversal of miniaturization in active follicles; minimal effect on fully miniaturized or kenogen follicles; no effect on scarred zones.

## §69. Restore Anagen and Lengthen Anagen Duration
- **Purpose.** Increase density and caliber by extending growth phase.
- **Biological target.** Dermal papilla signaling; vasculature.
- **Expected effect.** Partial density and caliber gain; effect ceiling depends on follicular reserve.

## §70. Reduce Perifollicular Inflammation (Non-Scarring)
- **Purpose.** Remove inflammatory amplification of miniaturization or shedding.
- **Biological target.** Perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate; sebaceous and microbiome contributors.
- **Expected effect.** Reduction of symptoms; reduction of shedding amplification; modest density preservation.

## §71. Suppress Cicatricial Inflammation
- **Purpose.** Halt extension of scarring alopecias.
- **Biological target.** Lymphocytic or neutrophilic perifollicular infiltrate at the bulge.
- **Expected effect.** Disease stabilization; preservation of unaffected follicles; **no recovery of scarred density.**

## §72. Restore Follicular Immune Privilege
- **Purpose.** Reverse or prevent recurrence in alopecia areata.
- **Biological target.** Peribulbar T-cell activity; cytokine milieu.
- **Expected effect.** Follicular reactivation; variable durability; relapse possible.

## §73. Normalize Endocrine Drivers
- **Purpose.** Remove endocrine amplifiers of miniaturization or cycling disruption.
- **Biological target.** Thyroid axis; prolactin; ovarian and adrenal androgens; insulin sensitivity; estrogen transitions.
- **Expected effect.** Substantial reversal of endocrine-driven shedding; partial effect on superimposed AGA.

## §74. Improve Nutrient Availability
- **Purpose.** Remove substrate limitations on cycling and keratinization.
- **Biological target.** Iron/ferritin; zinc; vitamin D; protein-energy; essential fatty acids.
- **Expected effect.** Substantial improvement when deficiency is real and documented; minimal effect in repletion-state individuals.

## §75. Stabilize Shedding
- **Purpose.** End an active telogen-shift episode.
- **Biological target.** The triggering insult (illness, medication, stressor, nutritional).
- **Expected effect.** Resolution of acute episode; recovery toward baseline density.

## §76. Improve Scalp Surface Health
- **Purpose.** Remove microbiome and barrier contributors to perifollicular inflammation.
- **Biological target.** *Malassezia*; sebum balance; barrier integrity.
- **Expected effect.** Symptom resolution; modest reduction in shedding amplification.

## §77. Reduce Mechanical Insult
- **Purpose.** Halt traction-driven injury before cicatricial conversion.
- **Biological target.** Hairstyle tension; cosmetic processing; pulling behaviors.
- **Expected effect.** Recovery if early; stabilization only if scarring has begun.

## §78. Preserve Follicular Function
- **Purpose.** Prevent further decline in any cause category.
- **Biological target.** Cumulative load on follicular niche.
- **Expected effect.** Slower decline; not a guarantee of improvement.

## §79. Address Psychogenic and Behavioral Drivers
- **Purpose.** Remove stress-driven amplification; address trichotillomania.
- **Biological target.** HPA axis chronic activation; pulling behaviors.
- **Expected effect.** Reduction of triggers; behavioral resolution preconditions follicular recovery.

## §80. Optimize Mechanical and Cosmetic Practice
- **Purpose.** Reduce avoidable shaft damage and traction.
- **Biological target.** Cuticle integrity; tension; thermal/chemical injury.
- **Expected effect.** Reduced breakage and apparent density loss; no direct effect on follicular biology.

# PART VIII — RECOVERY & REVERSIBILITY MODEL

## §81. Fully Reversible States

The following are biologically capable of full recovery toward baseline, given trigger resolution and an intact follicular niche:

- Acute telogen effluvium.
- Postpartum effluvium.
- Drug-induced telogen effluvium for most drug classes upon cessation.
- Anagen effluvium with bulge intact.
- Nutritional deficiency–related loss with adequacy restoration.
- Thyroid-related diffuse loss with euthyroid restoration.
- Hyperprolactinemia-related loss with cause control.
- Early-stage traction alopecia prior to cicatricial conversion.
- Tinea capitis with adequate antifungal therapy.

## §82. Partially Reversible States

The following may achieve meaningful improvement but not full restoration:

- Androgenetic alopecia, early to moderate, with sustained intervention.
- Female-pattern AGA with concurrent endocrine optimization.
- Chronic telogen effluvium with driver removal.
- Alopecia areata, particularly limited patchy forms (extensive forms have lower probability).
- PCOS-associated AGA with endocrine and AGA-directed combination.
- Seborrheic dermatitis–amplified loss superimposed on AGA (the amplified component is reversible).
- Senescent alopecia (modest gains achievable).

## §83. Irreversible States

The following are biologically irreversible at the follicular level once realized:

- Any zone exhibiting loss of follicular ostia.
- Frontal fibrosing alopecia in the scarred band.
- Lichen planopilaris in scarred patches.
- CCCA in scarred zones.
- Folliculitis decalvans in scarred zones.
- Dissecting cellulitis in scarred zones.
- Late traction alopecia with confirmed scarring.
- Advanced AGA with predominant vellus and kenogen state.

## §84. Biological Limits

Universal biological limits constrain every recovery objective:

- **Bulge stem cell pool** is finite and not regenerable through current biological intervention.
- **Dermal papilla cell number** below a critical threshold precludes terminal hair production.
- **Anagen duration** has a genetically conditioned ceiling.
- **Density** cannot exceed historical follicular complement; new follicles are not formed in adult human scalp under any currently validated intervention.
- **Scarred tissue** does not regenerate follicles.

## §85. Recovery Ceilings

Recovery ceiling is the maximum biologically attainable improvement for a given individual and cause configuration:

- **Acute self-limited causes.** Ceiling approaches pre-insult baseline.
- **AGA.** Ceiling is partial restoration of caliber and modest density in the still-cycling follicle pool; not regrowth in vellus-dominant or kenogen-dominant zones; not regrowth in scarred zones.
- **Scarring alopecias.** Ceiling is stabilization; the affected zone does not recover.
- **AA.** Ceiling is variable; many achieve substantial regrowth; severe and chronic forms have lower ceilings; relapse is possible.
- **Endocrine causes.** Ceiling approaches baseline with sustained endocrine control, modified by any concurrent AGA biology.

Stating a recovery objective above the ceiling is biologically and clinically indefensible.

# PART IX — OUTCOME EXPECTATION FRAMEWORK

This Part defines realistic, evidence-based outcome expectations. No marketing assumptions are permitted. Uncertainty is declared explicitly.

## §86. Stabilization Expectations
- **AGA.** With sustained appropriate intervention, stabilization is a realistic primary outcome in the majority of treated individuals over a 6–12-month horizon.
- **Scarring alopecias.** Stabilization is the primary realistic outcome; complete arrest is not guaranteed.
- **Chronic TE.** Stabilization expected upon resolution of driver; relapse possible.
- **AA.** Stabilization is unpredictable; the natural history includes spontaneous remission and spontaneous relapse.

## §87. Recovery Expectations

### §87.1 Timelines
- **Hair cycling** changes manifest visibly over **3–6 months** at minimum; meaningful caliber and density assessment requires **6–12 months**.
- **Acute TE recovery.** 6–12 months from trigger resolution.
- **Postpartum effluvium recovery.** 6–12 months postpartum.
- **AGA caliber/density response.** 6–12 months for early signal; 12–24 months for stabilized response.
- **AA regrowth.** Weeks to months for early response in limited disease; less predictable in extensive disease.
- **Endocrine restoration response.** 3–6 months after stable euthyroid or normalized endocrine state.

### §87.2 Magnitudes
- AGA interventions, on average, achieve modest caliber and density gains, not full restoration.
- Acute and self-limited causes typically achieve near-baseline recovery when triggers resolve and the niche is intact.
- Scarring alopecias do not achieve recovery in scarred zones under any current biological intervention.

## §88. Maintenance Expectations
- AGA outcomes are maintenance-dependent; cessation of intervention is typically followed by gradual return toward the untreated trajectory over months to years.
- Endocrine-driven outcomes require sustained control of the endocrine driver.
- Scarring alopecia outcomes require sustained suppression of inflammation.

## §89. Biological Uncertainty

Uncertainty is intrinsic and must be declared, not concealed. Sources include:

- Individual variability in genetic susceptibility and response.
- Imperfect knowledge of the precise upstream driver in a given individual.
- Multifactorial causation with variable per-individual contributions.
- Limits of clinical evidence in some subpopulations.
- Stochastic course of relapsing-remitting diseases.

When uncertainty is high, the system must state the uncertainty rather than asserting a confident outcome.

## §90. Prohibited Outcome Constructs
The following are prohibited under all circumstances:

- Guarantees of regrowth.
- Specific numeric promises ("X% regrowth in Y weeks").
- Comparative claims of superiority not supported by upstream evidence references.
- Urgency, scarcity, or fear framings of any kind.
- Implication of recovery in biologically irreversible zones.

# PART X — MONITORING SCIENCE

## §91. Meaningful Improvement Markers
- **Reduced shedding** sustained over weeks (validated by paired pull tests or hair-count proxies where available).
- **Caliber increase** measurable by trichoscopic assessment at the same anatomical landmarks.
- **Density increase** measurable in defined regions of interest.
- **Resolution of perifollicular erythema and scaling.**
- **Resolution of symptoms** (pruritus, burning, trichodynia).
- **Resolution of active patches** (in AA: cessation of expansion and emergence of regrowth).

## §92. Meaningful Deterioration Markers
- **Sustained excess shedding** beyond expected windows.
- **New patches** or expansion of existing patches.
- **New onset or extension of loss of ostia.**
- **New onset or worsening of perifollicular erythema or scaling.**
- **Caliber reduction** at landmark sites.
- **Migration of pattern** beyond expected stage.
- **New systemic signs** (thyroid, endocrine, autoimmune).

## §93. Reassessment Indicators
- Defined time interval since last assessment (per the Monitoring Registry).
- Patient-reported change exceeding declared thresholds.
- Trigger event (illness, surgery, medication change, parturition, severe stressor).
- Onset of new symptoms or new pattern features.

## §94. Escalation Indicators
- **New loss of follicular ostia** in any zone — escalation to clinician review.
- **Rapid expansion of patches** — escalation.
- **Pustules, sinus tracts, or systemic symptoms** — escalation.
- **Childhood patchy alopecia with scaling** — escalation (tinea capitis consideration).
- **Pregnancy, suspected pregnancy, or planning of pregnancy** — escalation for any intervention with reproductive implications.
- **Suspected drug-induced loss involving high-stakes medications** — escalation.
- **Loss accompanied by systemic illness signs** — escalation.
- **Patient-reported severe psychological distress** — escalation to appropriate support pathway.

# PART XI — CONTRAINDICATION & SAFETY MODEL

## §95. Biological Contraindications

A biological contraindication is a state in which an intervention's mechanism is incompatible with the patient's biology:

- Cicatricial alopecia in scarred zones — interventions targeting regrowth are biologically inappropriate as primary objectives.
- Active dermatophyte infection — anti-androgen or anti-inflammatory interventions are inappropriate prior to antifungal control.
- Active pustular folliculitis — regrowth-targeted interventions are inappropriate prior to inflammatory control.
- Anti-androgen interventions in genetic male individuals not carrying the AGA biology.
- Estrogenic strategies in individuals with relevant contraindicating conditions.

## §96. Contextual Contraindications

A contextual contraindication is a state in which the patient context, not the biology alone, precludes an intervention:

- Pregnancy or planned pregnancy — anti-androgens, retinoids, and certain other classes are contraindicated.
- Lactation — drug-class–dependent restrictions.
- Childhood — most pharmacologic alopecia interventions are not validated.
- Comorbid hepatic, renal, or cardiovascular conditions affecting specific drug classes.
- Drug-drug interactions of concern.
- Concurrent treatment of an active scarring disease — adjunct strategies must not interfere with primary disease control.

## §97. Escalation Conditions
Escalation to clinician review is required when:

- A scarring process is suspected or confirmed.
- Rapid progression is observed.
- Systemic signs accompany hair loss.
- Pregnancy considerations are present.
- High-stakes medications are implicated.
- Pediatric patients present with focal or diffuse loss.
- Psychological distress reaches a defined threshold.

## §98. Clinician-Review Triggers
Clinician review is mandatory before any clinical assertion or recommendation in the following situations:

- Suspected primary cicatricial alopecia.
- Suspected dissecting cellulitis or folliculitis decalvans.
- Suspected tinea capitis (especially pediatric).
- Suspected acute autoimmune onset.
- Suspected drug-induced loss requiring medication review.
- Any case in which the evidence base does not support deterministic resolution to a single dominant cause.

# PART XII — SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY RULES

## §99. Evidence Hierarchy

The evidence hierarchy applied throughout this model, in descending authority:

1. **Mechanistic biology established by replicated human studies.**
2. **Randomized controlled trials in the relevant population.**
3. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses.**
4. **Cohort and case-control studies in the relevant population.**
5. **Expert consensus guidelines from recognized dermatologic authorities.**
6. **Case series and small studies.**
7. **Mechanistic plausibility based on animal or in-vitro evidence.**
8. **Anecdote and uncontrolled reports.**

Tiers 7–8 may not, on their own, support a clinical assertion. They may motivate research but not recommendations.

## §100. Uncertainty Handling

Uncertainty must be:

- **Declared explicitly** at the point of assertion.
- **Quantified categorically** where possible (low, moderate, high).
- **Linked to evidence references** that justify the categorical level.
- **Preserved through downstream registries** rather than smoothed away.
- **Never converted to false confidence** for the sake of recommendation strength.

## §101. Conflicting Evidence Handling

When evidence is conflicting:

- The conflict must be explicitly recorded.
- The default canonical resolution prefers higher-tier evidence per §99.
- Where tier-equivalent evidence conflicts, the more conservative biological assertion prevails.
- Where the conservative assertion cannot be determined, the model declares the conflict and defers to clinician review.

## §102. Biological Plausibility Requirements

Every claim in the model must:

- Have a plausible biological mechanism by which it acts.
- Be consistent with established hair biology in Part I.
- Be consistent with the pathway and mechanism graph in Parts III and V.
- Be free of mechanistic contradictions with other parts of the model.

A claim that lacks biological plausibility is inadmissible regardless of any empirical association.

This model is valid only if it is:

- **Biologically coherent.** Internally consistent across all Parts; no contradictions between anatomy, cycle, signals, pathways, causes, mechanisms, objectives, reversibility, outcomes, monitoring, and safety.
- **Clinically defensible.** Every claim is supported by evidence drawn from the hierarchy in §99 at a tier compatible with the strength of the claim.
- **Explainable.** Every assertion is traceable to an upstream mechanism, signal, or cause; no opaque assertions remain.
- **Deterministic.** Identical inputs through the registries derived from this model produce identical outputs across independent implementations.
